# A retention experience customers can understand in one glance

**A practical checklist from the UseLoyalty team.** Loyalty software helps only when a customer knows how to join, what to do next, and why returning is worthwhile. A polished dashboard matters less than the customer journey it publishes.

## 1. Give the program one clear promise

Start with a sentence that names the action and the reward: “Visit five times and choose a free drink,” or “Finish the onboarding steps and unlock a useful account perk.” Avoid a generic promise to “earn points” with no visible destination. Set a reward cost you can sustain, then show the progress in customer language.

## 2. Make joining fit the moment

At a counter, a QR code should take someone straight to a mobile-friendly join screen. In an online product, the program should appear where the user finishes a useful action. On a storefront, post-purchase can be stronger than an interruptive popup. Ask for only the information needed to identify the member and honor the reward.

## 3. Show the next action, progress, and reward together

A useful member page answers three questions without a tutorial: What can I do now? How close am I? What will I get? Put the current quest or stamp card, point balance, and redeemable reward in the same view. If you use tiers, explain how status is earned and when it expires.

## 4. Protect trust when rewards fail

Test the awkward paths before launch: a missed visit, duplicate scan, expired reward, returned order, and a customer who changed devices. Staff need a way to look up a member and explain what happened. The program should show terms before the customer commits effort.

## 5. Measure repeat behavior, not vanity totals

Track active members, repeat visits or purchases, redemptions, and the cost of rewards. A high signup count with no redemption may mean the reward is too distant, the path is confusing, or reminders arrive at the wrong time. Try one change at a time and watch whether it improves useful repeat activity.
